Answer: The strangler-fig pattern for migrating a monolith replaces the system incrementally by intercepting calls at its edge and redirecting them capability by capability until nothing routes to the original monolith. ## Conclusion

Grounded QA works on this corpus, and the two fallback gates carry different weight than expected. Across the ten questions the v3 prompt answered nine and refused one, every answer carried at least one citation (9/9), no answer cited a chunk that was not supplied (0 fabricated), and no answer was produced from an empty context. The refusal is the result worth trusting least on its own, so it was measured against its counterfactual rather than asserted: case-1 runs q10 through the assignment's own starting prompt over the identical retrieved chunks and gets a confident eight-step tutorial on LoRA, Hugging Face and gradient clipping — not one word of which comes from a logistics corpus. The same question under v2 returns the refusal sentence. That single pair is the homework's core evidence: the retrieval was identical, so the hallucination was entirely the prompt's doing. 

The two gates did not contribute equally. The relevance floor fired exactly once (q10, top 0.266 against a 0.35 threshold) and the prompt rule never had to override a context it did receive — no in-corpus question was refused. But the floor is thinner than the aggregate suggests, and the per-question data says so: q05 cleared it at 0.412 on a foreign-document chunk the answer then did not cite, while the two chunks that actually answered scored 0.354 and 0.361 — 0.004 and 0.011 above the line. q04, the vocabulary-mismatch paraphrase HW2 flagged, cleared at 0.413. Two of the nine answered questions sit within 0.07 of refusal (q05 at +0.062 and q04 at +0.063) and a third, q06, within 0.09 (+0.081). The floor earned its place on the one query it was built for, and it is one recalibration away from costing a correct answer. 

What generation adds over retrieval is clearest where HW2's own complaints were sharpest. HW2 judged q09 unresolved because "the POD-to-settlement link is retrievable but not cleanly isolated in one chunk"; the model assembled it from three and cited all three. HW2 called q01's top-1 imprecise because an overlap tail pushed the definition to top-3; generation reads all of top-k, so the chunk boundary stopped mattering. And HW2/HW3's 0.582-vs-0.581 tie on q02 — which HW3 spent BM25 on breaking — turned out to be irrelevant once both chunks were in context. Several defects that were genuinely worth fixing at the retrieval grade simply stop being defects once a model reads the whole top-k. The inherited limits that do survive are the ones about content rather than ordering: q08's breadcrumb-inflated score still decides which chunks arrive, and no prompt rule can repair a chunk that was never retrieved.
